An email is a kind of delivery system made up of discrete messages that can be sent and received over the internet from two or more separate locations. Email messages can be transmitted through and from various software applications and web browsers, which are referred to as "email clients" and were created in the 1970s. However, for an email to reach the recipient's address, it must pass through several servers. Much like an actual letter or message, emails do indeed have addresses and the sender or recipient's name, however, the contrast lies in its headers and its functions. While much more efficient, it can be rather inconvenient as mobile devices or computers can run into numerous issues. For example, one might unexpectedly lose WiFi or netowrk, or simply crash. Luckily, most email systems provide drafts which are able to be saved and returned to at a much more convenient time. Email addresses at the end of the account's name is then distinguised by a string of unique characters, usually an "@" sign and a ".com" to help distinguish where it originates. All the while being very useful in everyday life by cutting costs, efficiency, and so much more.
